Flow Capture records what each Android touch hit, as the iOS agent does, so Match and Edit treat Android taps the way they treat iOS ones. For a boot the relay marks as a recorded person's (`observed` with `touchObservations`), the agent asks, just after each touch-down is sent, what the accessibility tree has at that point (`Hit` in the uidump jar, through the field server, which holds its UiAutomation connection while the person is recorded, clearing its node cache before each answer, and lets go of it for a tree dump), and 0.85 s after the lift reads the app's frames once (`dumpsys gfxinfo <pkg> framestats`). It sends one `capture:touched` per gesture on the app under test — the element and button named by the UI tree's rules, whether a list was still gliding under it (a fling just before, the app drawing up to the touch and stopping at it, a list there), whether the app drew in answer (`acted`, unknown wherever the frames could be someone else's), the double-tap count and the touch's number in the app's process, left out once a touch in that process may have gone unreported. A hit read after the up reached the app is dropped with its report. Touches on the launcher, the status bar or on another app's dialog get none; a key on the soft keyboard is reported as `surface: 'keyboard'`. In the dashboard, Match finds a named tap by scrolling the way the swipe before it went on the screen, also when an Android device held sideways sent that swipe in its frame's space. In flow-runner, `recordedTarget` and `recordedSelector` tell elements that share an Android resource id (`pkg:id/name`, one per row of a list) apart by the reported label, and name none when that label is gone, so the tap goes to its point and is compared; any other identifier still names its one element whatever its label says now.
